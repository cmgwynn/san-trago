What this concept proposes

This concept is original, code-drawn placeholder art. It is inspired by Stardew Valley's cozy, readable pixel style but copies none of its assets, UI, or characters. It is a direction to react to, not final art, and the creative lead owns the final call.

Scene, front to back

  • Workstation: stations on a wooden shelf in front of a Talavera tile wall, with the glass docked at the cola gun mid-pour (Mode C).
  • Bar top: a bevelled plank counter where guests rest their hands, with a coaster waiting for Maya's drink, Jules's beer, a tip jar, and a potted aloe.
  • Guests: three spots. Maya is selected and waiting, Jules is enjoying a beer and asking for peanuts, and spot 3 is open (a seagull is taking advantage).
  • Life: optional ambient animation, with clouds, waves, sun glitter, palms, and papel picado swaying, plus blinking, a pecking seagull, the cola stream, sink bubbles, and the washer counting down with steam. It is off by default for users who prefer reduced motion. State and selection never blink.
  • View: an open-front shack under a Mission tile roof, papel picado, fan palms, Crystal Pier-style cottages, a lifeguard tower, and the Point Loma lighthouse on the horizon.

Readability rules

  • Interactables get a dark warm outline (#3a2630); the background gets none and lower contrast.
  • Selection is a gold frame (bubble and docked glass), never only a color change.
  • State is always shown with an icon or number as well (hearts for patience, counts on tags, units on the gauge).
  • The pixel font is 5 px tall, which is 20 px on screen at x4. Nothing smaller.
  • All UI uses one parchment panel with a wood frame, so the HUD reads as one family.
  • Soft cast shadows under every object, plus a roof-eave shadow, anchor things in space without heavy outlines.

Screen 2: end of shift

  • Background: the same layers as service, recolored with a sunset palette (sun setting into the sea) and dimmed, so the bar visibly "closes" without new art.
  • The tab: service stats, then money with dotted leaders: drinks, tips in green, spills in red, and the total carried into savings ($128 to $252, matching the service HUD).
  • Guest notes: small portraits with one line each. Regulars, requests for tomorrow, and a seagull who stole peanuts give the numbers some heart.
  • Unlocked: previews what the shop and the next wave hold, so the Shop button has a reason to be pressed.
  • Choices: two large buttons, with the gold selection frame used everywhere else and a hint bar that also confirms the autosave.

What screen 2 tests

  • Font load: about 25 lines of 5 px text. If this feels tiring, that is the evidence for a crisper 2x HUD font on text-heavy screens.
  • Palette swaps: sunset is a recolor, not a repaint. If it holds up, day, sunset, and night can share one set of background art.
  • One UI family: panels, tags, buttons, and selection all reuse the service-screen parts.
  • Placeholder numbers: every figure is illustrative and not taken from the balance worksheet.

Palette

Very San Diego, on purpose

  • Washingtonia fan palms with brown frond skirts
  • Crystal Pier-style white cottages with blue trim
  • Point Loma lighthouse and headland on the horizon
  • Lifeguard tower, surfers, sailboats, seagulls
  • Mission terracotta roof, Talavera tile, papel picado
  • Surfboard tap handle; brown-and-gold baseball tee (no team logos)

Each neighborhood can swap this layer: Pacific Pour is beach and pier, and the others get their own skyline and tile.

Production implications (for review)

  • 16 px base grid; characters about 20 x 36 px; stations about 24 to 48 px wide.
  • Integer scaling only, with nearest-neighbor filtering in Godot (texture filter Nearest, stretch mode viewport).
  • The layout is 16:10. Letterbox other aspect ratios rather than stretching.
  • Background, guests, stations, and HUD are separate layers, so animation and neighborhood swaps stay independent.
  • The text-heavy screens (shop and results) may need a crisp HUD font at 2x. Decide before production.

Questions for the creative lead

  • Does pixel art at this density feel right, or should it be chunkier (fewer pixels) or finer?
  • Is daytime the default service look, with sunset or night as later waves or neighborhoods?
  • Are guests visible from the chest up, or should they be smaller full-body figures at stools?
  • Should the bubbles keep the recipe text, or switch to icons only with the recipe on hover?
  • Is the results screen comfortable to read in the 5 px font, or should text-heavy screens use a crisper 2x font?
  • Do the guest notes and the seagull joke fit the tone you want, or should results stay purely numbers?
